Live Update Analysis · November 16–17, 2026

WoW Forever November 16–17 Updates: Auction House, Dungeon XP & Economy

The newest live changes touch listing costs, dungeon leveling rules, a loot exploit correction, raid preparation, fishing, class mechanics and equipment. Here is what players should understand—not just what changed.

Reviewed November 19, 2026. This article summarizes the November 16–17 live changes and related recent adjustments. The game client and Blizzard's official posts are the final reference for current behavior.

1. Auction House: the minimum deposit is now lower

The minimum Auction House deposit was reduced from 1 silver to 1 copper with the November 17 maintenance deployment. Blizzard had announced the change earlier, then delayed it to the weekly maintenance after deployment issues.

Why it matters: lower minimum listing deposits reduce the upfront cost of testing low-value listings and selling inexpensive materials. It does not automatically make every auction cheaper to post—the deposit can still depend on item and listing details. Sellers should check the fee shown in the live Auction House window before listing.

Practical seller checklist

  • Confirm the actual deposit displayed for the item and duration.
  • For low-priced materials, compare the deposit with the expected sale value and competition.
  • Avoid repeatedly reposting without checking whether the item is selling or whether the price has changed.

2. Dungeon XP: the 50% increase now has a party-level safeguard

Blizzard increased experience from dungeon creature kills by 50% in the November 9 hotfix. A later adjustment addressed groups where high-level players made enemies trivial: the XP restriction applies when a party member is more than 12 levels above the enemy, rather than using the broader earlier interpretation.

What this means for leveling groups: dungeon runs can still be a useful leveling option, but party composition matters. If one character is far above the dungeon's enemies, the group may lose kill XP for those enemies. This is especially relevant to mixed-level friends and players joining runs with a high-level helper.

  • Check enemy levels throughout the dungeon; a single instance can contain a wide level range.
  • Keep the party within a sensible leveling range when kill XP is the goal.
  • Evaluate actual run time, travel, group formation and quest completion—not just the 50% XP headline.

See our live dungeon leveling guide for practical comparisons.

3. Blackmaw Hold: loot correction and economy enforcement

Blizzard explained that a large group of non-elite enemies in Blackmaw Hold had incorrectly been dropping loot at the level expected from elite creatures. The underlying issue was hotfixed, and Blizzard said it was reviewing the economic impact. Its November 17 statement distinguishes players who ran the dungeon a few times and benefited naturally from the bug from abuse of the unintended loot source.

Takeaway for farmers: a temporary loot pattern is not proof of a sustainable gold or material farm. Farming routes can be adjusted when a source is unusually lucrative, and unintended drops should not be treated as a reliable market baseline.

4. Camping and Fishing Rack quality-of-life changes

  • The Cozy Sleeping Bag can be placed in specific locations and now shows a Resting aura when players are within its effect area.
  • Players can no longer sleep while mounted.
  • The Fishing Rack now provides lures for everyone; Bloated Fish drop chance and loot tables were adjusted.

These are primarily usability and activity updates. For Fishing, verify the current rack effect and loot in-game before investing time or materials based on assumptions about the drop table.

5. Class and equipment changes worth checking

  • Enhancement Shaman: recent fixes and tuning changed Windfury Totem interactions, including armor behavior, internal cooldown and rage generation. Weapon-speed/proc behavior has also been normalized in recent updates.
  • Restoration Druid: Wild Growth was adjusted and its ranks share cooldown behavior; verify the current rank and cast rules before relying on older guides.
  • Shadow Priest: Vampiric Embrace party healing was reduced in the recent tuning cycle; recheck group-healing expectations.
  • Paladin: Judgement classification and itemization interactions have changed recently. The November 17 notes also adjusted tank Soulforge Armor toward Spell Power.
  • Warrior: recent fixes restored or corrected talent interactions on Cleave and Heroic Strike, and adjusted Windfury-related interactions.

These changes can affect talent choices, group roles and which gear is attractive. They are not enough by themselves to declare a class universally stronger or to predict a specific item's market price.

6. Upper Blackrock Spire difficulty restored

Blizzard corrected a bug that had made Upper Blackrock Spire enemies substantially weaker than intended. The developer note says the dungeon should again require a full, diligent group. Players planning runs should expect the intended encounter difficulty rather than relying on the temporarily reduced enemy health and damage.

What should players prioritize this week?

  1. Leveling: form a level-appropriate dungeon group and check whether kill XP is being restricted by party levels.
  2. Gold: test listing fees in the live Auction House and avoid assuming a farm remains profitable after an exploit correction.
  3. Professions: reassess low-value material listings now that minimum deposits are lower, but track actual sale prices and competition.
  4. Group preparation: revisit class guides and gear assumptions for the latest changes; prepare a coordinated group for Upper Blackrock Spire.
  5. Fishing and camping: confirm the updated Fishing Rack and Cozy Sleeping Bag effects in game.

Market note: these updates change some costs, rewards and farming conditions. They do not establish a guaranteed direction for gold, material or item prices. AzerothMarket will track confirmed changes separately from market observations.
